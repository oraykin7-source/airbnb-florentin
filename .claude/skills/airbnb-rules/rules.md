## The 12 rules that matter most for this listing

1. Nothing stay-related is paid outside Airbnb: no cash/Bit/PayPal for extra nights, late check-out, laundry, damage. Use "Change reservation" or the Resolution Center. (ToS 5.4, 11.1; Off-Platform policy; article 2827)
2. No off-platform links in the listing text or in pre-booking messages, and no direct-booking offers anywhere. The guide link goes only in post-confirmation messages / arrival guide; the site must never offer booking, pricing or "book direct". (Off-Platform policy 2799)
3. Before booking: all contact on Airbnb only; never ask for phone, email, WhatsApp or ID photos. After booking: WhatsApp is optional, never required; do not collect email/addresses or use contact data for marketing. (2799)
4. Do not "require or encourage Guests to open an account, leave a review, complete a survey, or otherwise interact, with a third party website, application or service". Affiliate links and the Airalo code stay on the website, labelled, optional, and never in Airbnb messages. (ToS 11.1; Content policy 546; Host privacy standards 2862)
5. No "commercial messages" via Airbnb messaging: no affiliate pitches, promo codes, product ads in listing, scheduled messages or review replies. (ToS 11.1)
6. Reviews: never incentivize, pressure, condition or reciprocate. No "5 stars or message me instead" wording; no perk for a review; never ask for a review off Airbnb. Asking for an honest review and feedback after check-out is fine (Airbnb recommends it). (Reviews policy 2673)
7. Listing must be complete and accurate: host lives here, shared bathroom, 1 guest, smart lock, hazards; every ticked amenity really exists; smoke/CO alarm answers must be true. Not disclosing the host's presence = "reservation issue", guest refund at host's cost. (ToS 5.1; 2895, 2343, 2868, 2904)
8. No indoor cameras or recording devices anywhere, even switched off. Noise monitors only in common areas and declared. Smart lock allowed; change the entry code between reservations. (3061, 2904)
9. House rules enforceable only via Airbnb's standard fields (guests, pets, smoking, events, quiet hours, check-in/out) plus "Additional rules"; no fees for skipped checkout chores; only the Resolution Center for real damage. (472, 2894)
10. Nondiscrimination: no restrictions by nationality, religion, age, family status etc.; "no children" not allowed in Israel; service animals accepted free even with "no pets". A live-in host sharing bathroom may restrict to guests of the host's own gender, applied to everyone. (2867, 1869)
11. Host cancellations cost 10% / 25% / 50% (min $50), lose payout and dates, risk Superhost; never ask the guest to cancel instead. Major Disruptive Events (war/hostilities, evacuation orders) only when Airbnb activates it for the area. (990, 1320)
12. Taxes: Airbnb collects none in Israel; the host is responsible for VAT/municipal duties; any guest-paid tax must be in the listing description. Not legal advice. (ToS; 2799)

### 1.8 What this means for a small host with a guest-guide link + affiliate links

- The single most relevant ToS line is 11.1: "Do not require or encourage Guests to open an account, leave a review, complete a survey, or otherwise interact, with a third party website, application or service before, during or after a Reservation, unless authorized by Airbnb." A guide link that is purely informational and optional is low risk; anything that *requires* or *pushes* the guest to sign up / buy / review on another site (GetYourGuide account, Airalo sign-up, Spa Plus booking) moves toward the prohibited zone. Keep every external link optional and presented as a convenience, never as a requirement for the stay.
- "Do not use the Airbnb Platform, our messaging tools, or Members' personal information to send commercial messages without their express consent." – Do not put affiliate pitches, promo codes or "book this tour via my link" in Airbnb messages or scheduled messages. The guide link itself in a message is a grey area; keep the message text about the stay, not about products.
- "Do not request, make, or accept a booking or any payment outside of the Airbnb Platform" – never collect anything from a guest in cash/Bit/PayPal for the stay, extra nights, late check-out, laundry, etc. Only the Resolution Center.
- Listing must be "complete and accurate" incl. price and rules, and you are "responsible and liable" for anyone you let help (cleaner, friend). You must carry your own insurance (5.7).
- Do not use Airbnb's name/logo on stayflorentin.com in a way that looks like Airbnb branding (11.1 "Honor your legal obligations" bullets 5–6).
- Never share account credentials (16).

## 2. Off-Platform and Fee Transparency Policy (+ "Offline Fee Policy")

- Title: Off-Platform and Fee Transparency Policy (Community policy)
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799
- Last updated: not shown on page
- Enforcement line: "By hosting on Airbnb, you agree to abide by the terms and policies, including the Terms of Service, which Airbnb reserves the right to enforce at its sole discretion. In the event of repeated or severe violations, Airbnb may suspend or permanently deactivate a user's listings or account. In order to protect the community, Airbnb prohibits the following behaviors."

### 2.1 Moving current, future, or repeat bookings off of Airbnb – "This includes:"
- "Asking or encouraging users to move current, future, or repeat bookings (including reservation extensions) off of Airbnb"
- "Including links that take people off of the Airbnb platform in listings or messages"
- "Offering or soliciting discounts to book off of Airbnb"
- "Canceling existing full or partial reservations to rebook off of Airbnb"

### 2.2 Failing to disclose reservation fees at the time of booking – "This includes:"
- "Failing to include any mandatory fees in the pricing fields provided by Airbnb or otherwise causing the total price at checkout to be inaccurate"
  - "Mandatory fees are costs that guests must pay given the number of nights booked, guest count, and pet count. Examples include utility fees, extra guest fees, pet fees, resort fees, management fees, destination fees, homeowners association (HOA) fees, and taxes (see exception below)."
  - "All mandatory fees must be disclosed in the appropriate fee field or the nightly price if there is no applicable fee field."
- "Failing to use Airbnb's "Change reservation" tool to collect payment for modifications to nights, guest count, or pet count"
- "Failing to disclose a security deposit. Most hosts are not allowed to charge a security deposit. For the small number of instances in which security deposits are permitted, they must be disclosed in the appropriate fee field."
- "Failing to disclose required deposits for incidentals as part of a hotel's standard procedures. The deposit amount must be disclosed in the listing description."
- Exception: "In locations where Airbnb does not collect taxes or where hosts are legally required to collect them directly from guests, hosts must disclose taxes in the listing description."

### 2.3 Payment for any reservation fee off of Airbnb
- "Requesting, sending, or receiving payments outside of Airbnb is prohibited. This includes the cost of the reservation and fee payments related to reservations (e.g., optional fee to heat the pool)."
- Exceptions: "Select software-connected hosts may collect payment for disclosed mandatory fees and security deposits off of the Airbnb platform"; hotels may take incidentals deposits / optional fees (parking) at check-in; "In locations where Airbnb does not collect taxes or where hosts are legally required to collect them directly from guests, hosts are permitted to collect disclosed taxes off of Airbnb".

### 2.4 Asking guests for, or using, contact or identity information in ways unrelated to their stay, or that compromise the quality of their stay – "This includes:"
- "Asking guests for contact information prior to booking; all guest communications prior to booking must be on Airbnb"
- "Soliciting guests for their email, mailing address, or other communications channels using the Airbnb messaging system or email alias after a booking"
- "Asking guests for contact information after a booking in order to run credit checks or background checks"
- "Asking guests to send photos of their government ID prior to arrival except where required for legal or compliance reasons as outlined below"
- "Using contact information provided by Airbnb for other purposes that violate the Terms of Service"
- "Selling, sharing, or using guest contact information for marketing communications or signing guests up for contact list"
- Exceptions:
  - "You may require additional contact/identity information if it is required for legal or compliance reasons and can be verified by a host upon request by Airbnb (such as local laws, HOA rules, building security rules). In such instances, hosts must include information about what is required and why in their listing description, so guests understand this additional step is a requirement prior to booking. Hosts are responsible for ensuring compliance with applicable data privacy laws"
  - "After accepting a booking, you may ask a guest to confirm that the contact information provided by Airbnb is an appropriate way to get in touch during their trip or if the guest requests an alternative or communication after booking"
  - "You may use an alternative means of communicating with a guest if requested by a guest after booking (ex: chat app), however, you must ensure such communications comply with the other requirements of this policy"

### 2.5 Taking people off of the Airbnb platform for feedback and reviews
- "You may not ask guests to review an Airbnb stay on a non-Airbnb website or fill out a survey regarding an Airbnb stay on a non-Airbnb website (such as a form off of Airbnb) unless you are an approved hotel partner. These actions take valuable input about a guest's stay away from the Airbnb community. Airbnb wants guests to share their feedback directly on Airbnb so that other guests may benefit from their insights."

### 2.6 Requiring guests to use other websites or apps to physically access their listing – "This includes:"
- "Asking guests to create a separate account or register on another website besides Airbnb.com for purposes of gaining entry to a listing"
- "Asking guests to install a third-party app to access a listing; all listings on Airbnb must be accessible to a guest without requiring they have another app or account"
- Exceptions: legal/compliance registrations (must be explained in the listing description); "Keyless entry apps and apps that facilitate a guest's experience during the stay (ex: Sonos, Nest, concierge apps) as long as they are optional"

### 2.7 "Offline Fee Policy" = Help article "Collecting fees outside Airbnb"
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2827 (this is what the ToS links to as "Offline Fee Policy"); no date shown
- "Hosts aren't permitted to collect any fees related to Airbnb reservations outside of our platform, unless expressly authorized by us."
- "In limited cases, Airbnb might permit some hosts, including select software-connected hosts, to collect certain mandatory fees..." – examples: resort fees (pool, gym, wifi), utility fees, HOA fees – and only if disclosed at checkout.
- Optional fees: "Hosts must collect payment for optional fees using the Resolution Center, though hotels may collect fees directly."
- Security deposits: "Most hosts aren't allowed to charge security deposits" (AirCover instead).
- Taxes may be collected directly only where Airbnb doesn't collect them or the law requires the host to.
- Hotels: credit-card holds for incidentals at check-in, disclosed resort fees, deposits released at/after checkout.

### 2.8 Before vs after booking confirmation – practical reading
- **Before booking (inquiry / request stage):** all communication must stay in Airbnb messaging. Do not ask for phone/email/WhatsApp. Do not put "links that take people off of the Airbnb platform in listings or messages" – this covers the listing text and pre-booking messages. Do not mention discounts for direct booking.
- **After confirmation:** Airbnb itself gives the guest the host's "contact number" and "full listing address" (help article 4116). You may ask the guest to confirm that the Airbnb-provided contact details work, and you may use WhatsApp etc. *if the guest asks for it*. You may not solicit their email/mailing address, may not add them to any mailing list, and may not use their contact details for marketing.
- The policy's link ban is written broadly ("Including links that take people off of the Airbnb platform in listings or messages") under the heading about moving bookings off-platform. A guide link (stayflorentin.com) is not a booking link, but it is an off-platform link in a message, so: send it only after confirmation, as a practical house-manual/arrival-info link, with no booking, pricing or "next time book direct" content anywhere on the site.

### 2.9 What this means for the Florentin host
- Never put stayflorentin.com in the public listing description or in pre-booking messages. Share it post-confirmation (e.g. in the check-in/arrival message), or better, paste the essential content into Airbnb's own House Manual / Guidebook.
- The site must not contain: any way to book or pay the room, any direct-booking discount, any form collecting guest email/phone, any review/survey form, any newsletter sign-up.
- Affiliate links (GetYourGuide, Spa Plus, Airalo) are not covered by the off-platform *booking* rule because they are not bookings of the accommodation – but they are "links that take people off of the Airbnb platform", and the ToS bans "encourag[ing] Guests to open an account… with a third party… service". Keep them optional, clearly labelled as affiliate/referral, and never require or push them in Airbnb messages.
- No cash/Bit/PayPal for anything stay-related (extra night, late checkout, laundry, towels). Use "Change reservation" or Resolution Center.
- Do not ask for ID photos before arrival (Israel has no such legal requirement for private hosts; if one ever applies, it must be in the listing description).
- Never ask a guest to review you on Google / the site / anywhere other than Airbnb.

- "Content that violates another person's or entity's rights, including intellectual property rights, privacy rights, or violations of our Copyright Policy"

### 3.3 Enforcement
- "Users are responsible for their content. We reserve the right to remove any content, in whole or part, that violates this policy" (plus the ToS and related policies). Serious or repeated violations can lead to account or listing suspension.
- Reporting: via the app or Airbnb support.

### 3.4 What this means for the Florentin host
- The listing text, photos, profile, messages and reviews are all "Content". The advertising rule is about content "created for the sole purpose of advertising a separate business… unrelated to the listing". A guide link that helps the guest with the stay is not that; a message or listing paragraph whose purpose is to push GetYourGuide/Airalo/Spa Plus is.
- Do not place affiliate links, promo codes, logos or company names in the listing description, house rules, profile, scheduled messages or review responses. Keep the affiliate content only on the website, and keep Airbnb messages about the stay.
- No photo with third-party logos/ads superimposed; no watermark advertising the site.
- Do not post anything that "is sufficient to identify a listing's location" publicly (reviews/responses/community posts) – consistent with the project rule that the address never appears in the repo or site.
- Don't send repeated/unsolicited messages (spam) – e.g. follow-up marketing after checkout.

## 4. Reviews Policy ("Authentic and trustworthy reviews") + review manipulation / extortion

- Title: Airbnb's Reviews Policy (page title "Authentic and trustworthy reviews")
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2673
- Last updated: not shown
- Principle: "reviews must be relevant, authentic, trustworthy, and follow our Content Policy."

### 4.1 What is not allowed – verbatim
**Reviews that are irrelevant**
- "Reviews should be about the offering reviewed. For example, if a review for a home is about a separate service or experience provided by the same host rather than the home, it isn't relevant to future guests deciding whether to book the home."
- "Reviews are irrelevant if they don't provide the participants' first-hand experience about the reservation."
- "If a guest never arrived for their reservation or chose to cancel due to circumstances unrelated to the offering they booked, their review will be considered irrelevant because it isn't based on first-hand experience of the offering."

**Reviews that are fake**
- "Reviews are fake if they aren't based on a real reservation. Reviews must be submitted by or on behalf of someone who participated in the reservation."

**Reviews involving bias, deception, extortion, incentivization, or pressure**
- "Reviews may not be used to mislead or deceive Airbnb or another person."
- "Hosts and guests may not coordinate with, manipulate, extort, incentivize, or pressure another person to influence a review or exploit the review system."
- "Hosts and guests may not threaten a negative review as a means to obtain unwarranted compensation, refund, or other incentive."
- "Reviews may not be provided or withheld in exchange for something of value—like a discount, refund, or a reciprocal positive review—or a promise not to take negative action against the reviewer."

**Reviews used to harm competition**
- "Hosts and guests may not leave reviews of listings with which they are directly affiliated or in direct competition."

**Reviews that are retaliatory**
- "Airbnb users may not write reviews to retaliate against another user for enforcing an Airbnb policy."
- "A review will only be considered retaliatory if the reviewer committed a policy violation, was notified of that violation, and then left a biased review because their own violation was reported. A review discussing the facts or legitimacy of a Resolution Center or AirCover claim isn't automatically considered retaliatory."

**Reviews that violate our Content Policy**
- "Reviews must follow our Content Policy. For example, reviews may not contain explicit, discriminatory, harmful, or illegal content."

### 4.2 Enforcement / removal
- "If a review violates this policy, we may remove that review, including any associated ratings and other content. Depending on the violation, we may also restrict, suspend, or remove the associated Airbnb listing or account."
- "Once a review you wrote is removed, you can not write a new one for that reservation."
- Does NOT qualify for removal: disagreement with a star rating (e.g. "Nice home and great stay" with 3 stars); factors outside the host's control ("the neighborhood is noisy"); subjective opinions ("The kitchen was small and cramped").
- "we generally don't mediate disputes concerning review accuracy. Instead, users can post responses to reviews that comply with this policy."
- Author can remove own review within 30 days of publication.
- Review moderation (article 3584): "Airbnb doesn't moderate reviews before they're published." Detection system removes reviews "likely to be fake—for example, because it doesn't relate to a genuine stay… or because it was solely made to inflate ratings".
- Mechanics (article 13): both sides are prompted "on the day of checkout" and have "14 days after checkout to submit their review"; reviews publish "after both parties have submitted their reviews, or once the 14-day period has ended—whichever comes first"; sub-category ratings stay private.

### 4.3 Related rules from other pages
- ToS 11.1: "Do not require or encourage Guests to … leave a review, complete a survey, or otherwise interact, with a third party website, application or service".
- Off-Platform policy: "You may not ask guests to review an Airbnb stay on a non-Airbnb website or fill out a survey regarding an Airbnb stay on a non-Airbnb website".
- Airbnb's own Resource Center guidance (Sep 17 / Dec 8, 2025) explicitly endorses: "Send a scheduled message at checkout" to "Ask guests for feedback, including specific ideas about how you could have made their experience even better", and "After departure: Thank guests for staying with you and request feedback 24 to 48 hours after they've checked out." Airbnb also notes that reviewing the guest within 14 days "reminds them to publicly review you." No official page provides template wording, and no official page explicitly bans the words "5 stars" – the ban is on incentivizing, pressuring, conditioning or coordinating.

### 4.4 What a host may / may not say when asking for a review
MAY:
- Thank the guest and remind them a review exists / ask them to leave an honest review on Airbnb (Airbnb's own Resource Center recommends this).
- Ask for feedback and improvement ideas (in Airbnb messaging).
- Say that you have left them a review (true statement, no condition).
- Invite the guest to tell you about any problem during the stay so you can fix it – as a hospitality message, during the stay, not tied to the review.

MAY NOT:
- Offer anything of value for a review (discount, refund, free night, gift, Airalo credit, affiliate perk): "Reviews may not be provided or withheld in exchange for something of value".
- Offer a reciprocal review ("I'll give you 5 stars if you give me 5"): "reciprocal positive review" is named explicitly.
- Pressure or repeatedly nag: "may not … pressure another person to influence a review"; repeated messages are also "spam" under the Content Policy.
- Condition the review on a rating ("only review if you can give 5 stars"; "please give 5 stars or tell me privately first") – this is coordination/manipulation of the review system and "withheld in exchange for … a promise". The common phrase "if anything was less than 5 stars please message me instead of reviewing" is a request to withhold a review and should not be used.
- Ask the guest to review/survey anywhere other than Airbnb (Google, the website, a form).
- Threaten a bad review of the guest, or threaten anything, to get a better review or compensation.
- Write/arrange reviews from friends, non-guests, or affiliated people; review competitors.
- Dispute a review just because the rating is low or the opinion is subjective – it will not be removed.

### 4.5 What this means for the Florentin host
- A post-checkout scheduled message like "Thanks for staying – if you have a moment, an honest review on Airbnb helps a lot; and I'd love any feedback on how to improve" is within the rules. Do not add "5 stars", do not add any perk, and do not add the website link or any affiliate content to that message.
- Never offer the Airalo code, a GetYourGuide "tip", or a discount on a future stay as thanks for a review.
- Do not put a review request, feedback form or "rate us" widget on stayflorentin.com.
- Keep the website guide strictly about the stay and the neighbourhood, so a review never reads as being about a "separate service" the host sells.

## 10-line summary of the most important prohibitions

1. No booking or payment of anything stay-related outside Airbnb (no cash/Bit/PayPal for extras; use Change reservation / Resolution Center) – ToS 11.1, Off-Platform policy, article 2827.
2. No off-platform links "in listings or messages" and no direct-booking discounts – Off-Platform policy; the guide link belongs only in post-confirmation arrival info, and the site must have no booking/pricing/direct-deal content.
3. Before booking: all communication on Airbnb only; never ask for phone, email, WhatsApp or ID photos – Off-Platform policy.
4. After booking: no soliciting email/mailing address, no mailing lists, no marketing use of guest contact data; WhatsApp only if the guest asks – Off-Platform policy.
5. Do not "require or encourage Guests to open an account, leave a review, complete a survey, or otherwise interact, with a third party website, application or service" – ToS 11.1; affiliate/referral links must stay optional and never be pushed in Airbnb messages.
6. No "commercial messages without their express consent" via Airbnb messaging – ToS 11.1; no affiliate pitches, promo codes or product ads in listing, scheduled messages or review responses.
7. No content "created for the sole purpose of advertising a separate business" (logos, links, company names) in listing/profile/photos/messages/reviews – Content Policy.
8. Reviews: never incentivize, pressure, coordinate, condition or reciprocate ("something of value… discount, refund, or a reciprocal positive review"); no "5 stars or message me instead" wording – Reviews Policy.
9. Never ask for a review or survey off Airbnb (Google, website, form) – Off-Platform policy + ToS 11.1.
10. Listing and price must be "complete and accurate"; all mandatory fees in Airbnb's fee fields; no security deposit; never publish anything "sufficient to identify a listing's location" – ToS 5.1, Off-Platform policy, Content Policy.

## 1. Ground rules for home hosts

- **Title:** Ground rules for home hosts (Community policy)
- **URL:** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895
- **Date shown:** none
- Related summary page: "What's expected of hosts" https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3057

### Rules (quoted)

Four areas: "Listing accuracy / Honoring reservations and refunds / Timely communication / Listing cleanliness". Also: "Repeated low ratings indicate that a listing or host may not be meeting guest expectations of quality."

**Listing accuracy** – "The listing page at the time of booking and any additional host statements about the listing should accurately describe the home and reflect the features and amenities that will be available at the listing from check-in to checkout":
- Booking details: "Hosts should only change the details of an accepted booking (dates, price, etc.) with the guest's prior consent."
- Location: "The location information (map pin, address, etc.) on the listing page should be accurate. The listing page should also disclose any information about the surroundings that may impact the expected level of noise."
- Type, size, and privacy: "The listing page should accurately describe the type of accommodation offered (private room, entire home, etc.), the setup of the listing (number of bedrooms, size of beds, etc.), and the level of privacy (presence of an on-site property manager, other guests, etc.)."
- Property: "the photos and description on the listing page should accurately represent the space provided". "We will ask hosts to remove content if AI or other digital technology has been used to edit flaws, hide damage, add amenities or attributes that are not part of a listing, or otherwise misrepresent the listing."
- Amenities: "The listing page should accurately represent all available amenities ... If the listing advertises 'essential amenities', all amenities from this list should be available to guests. If there are restrictions associated with amenity access, these should also be fully disclosed on the listing page".

**Honoring reservations and refunds**
- "Hosts should not cancel confirmed reservations, unless there are certain valid reasons beyond the host's control that make hosting impossible. ... Hosts may not ask the guest to cancel the reservation on their behalf."
- Check-in: "Hosts should provide their guests with the ability to readily access the listing at check-in (ex: correct directions, updated keycode, etc.) and throughout their stay."
- "Hosts should honor their promises to refund or compensate guests ... in a timely manner."

**Timely communication** (for a question "material to their stay"):
- "If check-in is more than five days away, hosts are expected to respond to guests within three days of receiving a message."
- "Close to check-in time, or if a time-sensitive issue arises during the stay ... hosts are expected to respond within 1 hour to guest messages received during local daytime hours. Airbnb may provide immediate assistance to guests who are facing a trip issue if the host is unresponsive."
- "when a guest reaches out with other inquiries during the stay or within five days of their arrival, hosts are expected to respond within 12 hours to guest messages received during local daytime hours."

**Listing cleanliness**
- "All listings should be clean and free of health hazards before check-in."
- "Listings should be free of health hazards (mold, pests, etc.)."
- "Hosts should provide listings that meet a high standard of cleanliness (free of extensive dust, pet dander, dirty dishes, etc.)."
- "Hosts should be sure to clean between every stay (do laundry, take out trash, vacuum/sweep, wipe down surfaces, etc.)."

**Enforcement:** "educating hosts about this policy, issuing warnings, and requiring hosts to remove content that misrepresents the listing". Repeated/severe: "hosts, co-hosts, or listings may be suspended or removed from the platform, hosts may be prevented from publishing new listings or lose their Superhost status ... and the timing of host and co-host payouts may be adjusted." Also "canceling an upcoming or active reservation, refunding a guest from a host's payout, and/or requiring hosts to provide proof that they have addressed issues before they can resume hosting." Appeals possible by contacting Airbnb.

### What this means for this host
- The listing must say clearly: private room, host lives in the apartment, bathroom shared with the host, and what (if anything) else is shared (kitchen, living room). Not disclosing that the host is present is treated as "significantly different than advertised" (see §5).
- Florentin is a noisy nightlife area: the listing should mention street/bar noise ("disclose any information about the surroundings that may impact the expected level of noise").
- Keep the door code working and sent on time; "updated keycode" is explicitly a ground-rule item.
- Reply targets: 1 hour (daytime) on check-in day and for urgent issues; 12 hours in the 5 days before arrival and during the stay; 3 days earlier than that.
- Clean between every guest, including laundry and bathroom; mold/pests are a policy violation, not just a bad review.
- Never alter dates/price of an accepted booking without the guest's consent, and never ask a guest to cancel for you.

## 2. Ground rules for home guests (what the host may expect and enforce)

- **Title:** Ground rules for home guests (Community policy)
- **URL:** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2894 (the brief's 3146 is wrong, see note at top)
- **Date shown:** none
- Related: "What's expected of guests" https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3053; Community Disturbance Policy https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3345

### Rules (quoted)

Two principles: "Treat your host's home like your own" and "Follow the host's standard house rules".

**Treat the home like your own**
- Cleanliness: "Guests should not leave the listing in a state that requires excessive or deep cleaning ... Cleaning fees set by hosts are only meant to cover the cost of standard cleaning between reservations (laundry, vacuuming, etc.). **Hosts may not charge guest fees for failing to perform specific cleaning or checkout tasks**, but they may leave ratings and reviews based on cleanliness."
- Litter: "Guests should put their trash in designated trash receptacles".
- Damage: "Guests are expected to pay reasonable requests for reimbursement if they're responsible for damage, missing items, or unexpected cleaning costs."

**Follow the host's standard house rules**
- Approved guests: "Guests should respect the approved number of guests and should ask their host if they're unsure about the rules for visitors. Disruptive gatherings are always prohibited."
- Check-in time: "Guests should respect their host's check-in window and should not check in before or after the designated window without prior approval from the host."
- Checkout time: "Guests should complete checkout, including key return, by the designated checkout time indicated on the Airbnb reservation, and should not leave belongings at the listing past the designated checkout time for storage or later pickup without prior approval from the host."
- Smoking: "Guests should respect 'no smoking' rules ... This includes the use of tobacco, cannabis, e-cigarettes, etc."
- Pets: "Guests should not bring any pets inside a listing that is designated as 'no pets' ... Note that service animals are not considered pets."
- Noise: "Guests should respect designated quiet hours and should not disturb the surrounding community with a disruptive level of noise".
- Commercial film/photography: not without "documented permission from the host".

**Additional rules:** "In addition to standard house rules covered in these ground rules, hosts can also add a written set of additional rules to their listing page. If a guest violates any of these additional rules, hosts can reach out to customer support to help with the appropriate next steps."

**Reporting a violation** – Airbnb asks hosts to: "Communicate with the guest", "Document the issue through the Airbnb message thread, an emailed complaint from a neighbor, photos, etc.", "Contact us to report any issues, or request payment for damages through the Resolution Center", "Leave an honest review".

**Enforcement on guests:** information, warnings; "When repeated or severe violations of these ground rules are reported, guests may also be suspended or removed from our community."

Community Disturbance Policy (3345): "we do not allow open-invite parties, disruptive gatherings, or other community disturbances during an Airbnb reservation"; signs include "Excessive noise", "Excessive trash", "Trespassing", "Smoking nuisances".

### What this means for this host
- The host may enforce: 1 guest max, no visitors without permission, no early check-in / late check-out without approval, no smoking (incl. vaping and cannabis), quiet hours, no pets (except service animals), no storing luggage after 12:00 without approval.
- The host may NOT bill guests for skipping checkout chores (e.g., "strip the bed or pay 50 NIS"). Checkout requests must be phrased as requests; the only remedy is the review and, for real damage, the Resolution Center.
- Keep all enforcement inside the Airbnb message thread (document there), not only WhatsApp, so a report is supportable.
- Disputes over damage go through the Resolution Center with photos; set no "penalty fees" in the house rules.

## 3. House rules: what hosts can and cannot put in them

- **Title:** Add house rules to a listing (How-to, Home host)
- **URL:** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/472
- **Date shown:** none
- Related: Update your house rules to share which services you allow https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3889; Accessibility Policy https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1869

### Rules (quoted)

Standard house rule categories: "Pets / Events / Smoking, vaping, and e-cigarettes / Quiet hours / Check-in and checkout times / Check-in method / Maximum number of guests / Commercial photography and filming / Allowing all, some, or none of the service categories in your home".

Additional rules: "If you have special requirements that aren't included in the set of standard house rules, you can write them under additional rules. Any additional rules that you set shouldn't contradict your standard house rules, such as check-in time. It's best to avoid overwhelming guests with too many rules, but you can add anything important about local customs or health and safety."

Where rules appear: "Your house rules are featured in your listing description, in booking terms while booking, in automated guest emails, and in the Arrival Guide guests receive ahead of their trip." Guests can view them "in the app by navigating to the Trips tab."

Airbnb Services in the home: "As a home host, you can choose which services you'll allow in your home. Once you decide, contact us to update your house rules" (chefs, photography, catering, massage, spa, hair, makeup, nails, personal training; "Prepared meals ... are allowed for all home listings since those are delivered").

Service animals: "Service animals, like guide dogs, always stay for free." (Accessibility Policy 1869: a host "may only ask" two questions – "Do you require your animal because of a disability?" and "What work or task has the animal been trained to perform?" – and "hosts should not ask for or require such documentation". With a service animal hosts may not "Refuse a reservation (unless an Airbnb-granted Exemption applies)", "Charge the guest additional fees", or impose "different house rules". Emotional support animals: outside CA/NY/Quebec/Ontario "a host may charge normal pet fees ... or decline a reservation if they don't accept pets." Exemption example: "if the presence of a Service Animal constitutes a direct threat to a host's health if the host lives in that listing" – must be requested proactively from Airbnb.)

Breaking rules: "If a guest breaks your rules, contact us to report any issues or request payment for damages through the Resolution Center."

What is enforceable (cross-reference): standard rules are backed by the guest ground rules (§2). Additional rules are enforceable only via "customer support to help with the appropriate next steps" (2894). Rules that are *not* allowed: fees for checkout tasks (2894); any rule that treats guests differently on a protected characteristic, e.g. "no guests under 21" outside the allowed cases (2867, §6); rules that contradict the listing's own check-in/out settings (472).

### What this means for this host
- Put the hard constraints in the standard fields (max 1 guest, no pets, no smoking, no events, quiet hours, check-in 14:00 / check-out 12:00, check-in method = code lock). These are the ones Airbnb enforces most directly.
- Use "Additional rules" for things specific to a shared flat: shared-bathroom etiquette, kitchen use, visitors policy, keeping the door code private, no shoes/noise after X, Shabbat/holiday notes if relevant ("local customs or health and safety" are explicitly welcome). Keep it short.
- The guest-guide website is fine as an expanded explanation, but anything the host wants to *enforce* must also be in the Airbnb house rules, because only those appear "in booking terms while booking" and in the Trips tab. A rule that lives only on the website is not something the guest agreed to.
- Do not write monetary penalties, "no guests under X years", or any gender/nationality/religion preference. If a one-person flat-share must be same-gender, see §6 (allowed only because the bathroom/kitchen are shared, and must be stated as the host's own gender).
- Times in additional rules must match the standard check-in/out fields exactly (14:00 / 12:00).

## 4. Security cameras, recording devices, noise monitors, smart locks, host-on-site

- **Title:** Restrictions on security cameras and other devices in homes (Community policy)
- **URL:** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3061
- **Date shown:** "These rules took effect on April 30, 2024."
- Companion how-to: Informing guests about security devices https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2914 (no date)
- Lock/code rule: Safety requirements for homes https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2904
- Host-on-site disclosure: Ground rules 2895 ("level of privacy") and Rebooking and refund policy https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2868

### Rules (quoted)

**Indoor cameras – total ban, including shared spaces of private rooms**
- "We do not allow hosts to have security cameras or recording devices that monitor indoor spaces in homes, even if these devices are turned off. Hidden cameras have always been prohibited and will continue to be prohibited."
- "Security cameras and recording devices are any device that records or transmits video, images, or audio such as a baby monitor, or doorbell camera."
- "Hosts are not allowed to have security cameras and recording devices that monitor any part of a home's interior, such as the hallway, bedroom, bathroom, living room, or guest house, even if they're turned off or disconnected. **These prohibitions also apply to the common areas and shared spaces of private room listings (ex: a living room).**"

**Outdoor cameras – allowed with disclosure**
- "Hosts are allowed to have exterior security cameras and recording devices, and are required to make sure their location is disclosed in the listing's description (ex: 'I have a camera in my front yard,' ... or 'I have a doorbell camera monitoring my front door and the hallway of my apartment building.')"
- "Home hosts are required to disclose exterior security cameras and recording devices under their control, such as the hallway of their apartment building or over their backyard."
- Not allowed where there is "a greater expectation of privacy, such as the inside of an enclosed outdoor shower or in a sauna."
- Disclosure path: Listing editor > Your space > Guest safety > Safety devices > "Exterior security camera present" – "Describe each device, its location ... and if it will be switched on or off".

**Noise decibel monitors**
- "Noise decibel monitors are devices that assess sound levels and their duration but do not record audio."
- "Home hosts are allowed to have noise decibel monitors in the home's interior as long as the host discloses their presence and they are not located in bedrooms, bathrooms, or sleeping areas. Hosts are not required to disclose where their noise decibel monitors are located."
- Disclosure path: Guest safety > Safety devices > "Noise decibel monitor present".

**Smart home devices (incl. smart locks, voice assistants)**
- "Smart home devices are devices that connect and interact with other devices or networks, such as Amazon's Alexa and Google's Nest."
- "Home hosts are allowed to have smart home devices."
- "Home hosts are encouraged, but not required, to disclose their presence in their listing. We also encourage hosts to give guests the option of unplugging or disabling smart devices."

**Locks and codes (2904)**
- "Keys or access codes should be provided for the main point of entry. Lockboxes and entry codes must remain secure, and hosts should change codes between each reservation."

**Host living on site – must be disclosed**
- 2895: listing must describe "the level of privacy (presence of an on-site property manager, other guests, etc.)".
- 2868 (Rebooking and refund policy, effective February 6, 2025) counts as a refundable "Reservation Issue": "The Listing fails to disclose that the host, another person, or a pet will be present during the stay."
- 317: "Specify who's present at your room listing ... Hosts can specify who is at their place when adding a new listing or in the description of their place."
- 3400 (Listing a Room): "The listing's Host must be a person using their own name—no business names, nicknames, or other names are permitted—because guests are sharing your home".

### What this means for this host
- No camera of any kind inside the apartment – not in the hallway, kitchen, living room, or anywhere the guest can be – even switched off, even a baby monitor or an indoor "doorbell" unit that sees inside. Remove or do not install.
- A camera/doorbell camera on the building stairwell or the apartment's outer door that the host controls is allowed only if disclosed in the listing with its location. A building-management camera not under the host's control is not the host's to disclose, but mentioning it is harmless.
- The code lock is a "smart home device" if connected; disclosure is encouraged, not required. Practical rule: change the code between guests (safety requirement) and never publish the code on the website or in the listing.
- A noise monitor (e.g., Minut) is allowed in the living room/kitchen only, never in the guest room or bathroom, and must be ticked in Guest safety > Safety devices.
- The listing must say the host lives in the apartment and will be present during the stay; if a pet or another person lives there, say so. Omitting this gives the guest a refund right.
- The listing must be under the host's real personal name to appear as a "Room".

## 5. Listing accuracy, photos, amenities, shared spaces, safety disclosures

- **Titles / URLs:**
  - Ground rules for home hosts (listing accuracy section) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895
  - Rebooking and refund policy for homes https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2868 – "Effective Date: February 6, 2025"
  - Providing essential amenities for guests https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2343

  - Listing a Room https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3400

  - Safety requirements for homes https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2904

  - Updating listing descriptions after a guest has booked https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1506
  - Confirming photo accuracy for listings https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2881
  - (none of these except 2868 show a date)

### Rules (quoted)

**What counts as "significantly different than advertised" (2868 – guest gets refund/rebooking, host loses payout):**
- "Inaccurate space type (ex: entire place, private room, or shared room)."
- "Inaccurate type or number of rooms (ex: bedrooms, bathrooms, and kitchens)."
- "Inaccurate location of the accommodations."
- "The Listing fails to disclose that the host, another person, or a pet will be present during the stay."
- "Special amenity or feature advertised in the Listing is not present or does not function (ex: pool, hot tub, major appliance, heating, and air conditioning systems)."
- Also a Reservation Issue: "Accommodations are not habitable at check-in ... Not reasonably clean and sanitary. Contain safety or health hazards." and "Host fails to provide their guest access to the accommodations."
- Guests "must report Reservation Issues within 72 hours after discovery"; "the host will receive no payout or will have their payout reduced by the amount refunded".

**Essential amenities (2343):** "We strongly encourage Hosts to provide these essential amenities in all their listings: Toilet paper / Soap (for hands and body) / One towel per guest / One pillow per guest / Linens for each guest bed". "Listings that have the essential amenities option selected but don't actually provide the items may be subject to penalties, including removal from Airbnb."

**Room / shared spaces (317, 3400, 3424):**
- Room: "Guests will have their own private bedroom with a door for sleeping. Other areas, such as a kitchen, living room, or bathroom could be shared. Bathrooms can be shared, private and attached, or dedicated exclusively to the guest. You can note this in your Listing details".
- Room requirements: "The space listed is a bedroom, with a door, that is intended to be exclusively used by the guest during their stay"; "The guest shares access to at least one of these common spaces: bathroom, kitchen, or living room"; host "must be a person using their own name".
- Bathroom privacy definitions: "Shared: The bathroom may be shared with other occupants of the house, for example, the host or other guests." Also: "guests expect the privacy of an interior lock or latch." Set in Listing editor > Photo tour > room > Privacy info.

**Safety (2904):**
- "Hosts should clearly communicate with guests about the specific attributes of each listing, including by completing the safety section of their listing description."
- "Keys or access codes should be provided for the main point of entry. Lockboxes and entry codes must remain secure, and hosts should change codes between each reservation."
- "Maintain smoke and carbon monoxide (CO) detectors. Hosts should clearly state whether these devices are present in a listing. If they are, detectors need to be in working order and hosts should regularly test the alarms."
- "hosts must address known hazards that could cause injury before accepting reservations" – e.g. "blocked exits, inoperable smoke detectors, or exposed wiring", "gas-powered kitchen appliances", "mold, or pests", "unsafe walls, ceilings, or windows".
- "hosts must disclose any safety hazards inherent to the listing by including them in the listing description (ex: access to high heights, a pool, or a body of water)."
- "hosts must disclose and take safety measures to secure weapons and certain types of animals present at a listing."
- "Hosts are accountable for violations by their co-hosts, employees, and others who they allow to assist in their hosting."
- 514: "If your place only uses electric appliances, and does not have an attached garage, you have the option to select that a carbon monoxide alarm is not necessary." Airbnb "provides a self-contained, battery powered, combination smoke and carbon monoxide alarm to eligible hosts at no cost."

**Changing the listing after booking (1506):** "guests with pending or confirmed reservations won't see any updates or changes you've made to your listing." "If you've removed an amenity your upcoming guest was expecting, message your guest immediately. If they no longer wish to keep the reservation, you'll be responsible for cancelling it".

**Photos (2881):** Airbnb may ask hosts to "take new photos of how their space is currently to match what's listed", in person via the app; "The short list of images listed as verified by Airbnb should represent the key features and amenities that guests expect upon arrival."

### What this means for this host
- Listing checklist: type = Room; bathroom = Shared (set in Photo tour privacy info); text says host lives there and is present; any pet/other resident named; map pin correct; street-noise note; bed size; which shared spaces the guest may use (kitchen? living room? balcony?) and any restrictions ("kitchen available 07:00–22:00" is fine if disclosed).
- Do not tick "Essentials" unless toilet paper, soap, a towel, a pillow and bed linen are always provided. Do not tick any amenity (AC, washer, workspace, etc.) that the guest cannot actually use.
- Safety section: declare whether there is a smoke alarm and CO alarm. If the kitchen has a gas hob/water heater, a CO alarm is strongly expected; if all-electric, the "not necessary" option may be chosen. Add a bathroom latch if missing (guests "expect the privacy of an interior lock or latch").
- Mention any inherent hazard on the website AND the listing (e.g., balcony/roof access, steep stairs, no elevator).
- If something breaks (AC, hot water, washer) before a stay, message the guest immediately; if they want out, the host must cancel (and then §7 applies, though a valid reason may waive fees).
- Photos must match the room as it is now; do not retouch away wear or add items.

## 6. Nondiscrimination Policy

- **Title:** Nondiscrimination Policy (Community policy)
- **URL:** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2867
- **Date shown:** "Last updated: March 24, 2026"
- Related: Accessibility Policy https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1869; Questions about the Nondiscrimination Policy https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1435

### Rules (quoted)

Protected characteristics: "Race / Religion / Gender / Age / Disability / Familial status (having children) / Marital status (being married or not) / Ethnicity / Nation of origin / Sexual orientation / Sex / Gender identity / Caste / Pregnancy and related medical conditions".

"Airbnb users may not treat members of the Airbnb community differently or deny service to someone because of their protected characteristics or the perception that they have a protected characteristic. Some examples of this include: Declining or canceling a booking. Imposing different terms, conditions, or house rules ... Indicating a preference for or against a specific type of guest."

"hosts are welcome to provide information about the home ... but ultimately the decision as to whether a listing is appropriate for a guest, their family, or their co-travellers, is up to the guest."

**Age / family:** hosts may not "Decide for guests that a listing does not meet the needs of guests of a certain age or guests with children or infants" nor impose "rules like 'no guests under 21' except where allowed by this policy". Age minimums for the *booking guest* (up to 25) are allowed only "For homes in the United States (including Puerto Rico) and other specified countries" (Israel is not named). Globally: "for listings with open nudity or overtly sexually explicit content or items ... hosts must state an age minimum of at least 18+". Hosts may "Provide factually accurate information about their home ... that could lead a guest to determine that the listing is unsuitable for guests of a certain age."

**Disability:** hosts may not "Charge more fees for guests with disabilities, including pet fees when the guest has a Service Animal", "Discourage bookings from guests with disabilities", or "Deny reservation requests to avoid reasonable adjustment requests ... (such as a minor change in house rules)". Hosts may "Provide information about a listing's accessibility features (or lack of them)".

**Gender identity:** hosts may not "Deny a reservation or impose differential treatment because a host disagrees with the expressed gender identity of a guest". Hosts may "**Make a home available only to guests of the host's gender if the host shares common spaces (ex: bathroom, kitchen) with their guests.**"

**Discriminatory language / symbols:** no slurs, stereotypes, "misgendering or deadnaming"; no "symbols, objects, logos, slogans, or images that are hateful, stereotype people because of a protected characteristic, or convey a discriminatory meaning."

**Local law:** "Hosts are allowed to explain legal restrictions that are important for guests to know in a clear, factual and non-derogatory manner." Hosts are not required "to accept guests that could expose the hosts to a real and demonstrable risk of legal liability or physical harm."

**Enforcement:** "educating users on our policies, issuing warnings, suspending or removing listings, or suspending or removing the violating accounts." Airbnb also "reserve[s] the right to take action for violations of this policy that occur off of the Airbnb platform."

### What this means for this host
- Decline or accept requests only on neutral grounds (dates, number of guests, the guest's stated plans conflicting with house rules, reviews). Never cite nationality, religion, age, family status, orientation, or gender identity, and do not write a "preferred guest" profile in the listing.
- The one allowed exception for a live-in host with a shared bathroom/kitchen: the listing may be restricted to guests of the host's own gender. If used, state it plainly ("Because I share the bathroom and kitchen, this room is for [male/female] guests only") and apply it to everyone.
- "No children" is not allowed as a rule; instead state facts ("single bed, shared flat, no cot available, 4th floor without elevator") and let the guest decide. "1 guest max" is fine.
- Service animals must be accepted with no fee even with "no pets". The host may ask only the two permitted questions. If a dog in the shared flat is a genuine health threat to the host (e.g., severe allergy), request an exemption from Airbnb in advance and then mention it in the listing.
- Keep house rules and website wording neutral; mention legal/cultural points (e.g., Shabbat elevator, holiday noise) factually, never as a preference about who stays.

## 7. Cancellations: host penalties, guest cancellation policies, Major Disruptive Events

- **Host Cancellation Policy for homes** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/990 – "Effective date: October 9, 2023"
- **Host cancellations** (overview) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/4117 – no date
- **Cancel a reservation as a host** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/166 – no date
- **Cancellation policies for your home** (guest-side policies you choose) https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/475 – no date (mentions "Limited" policy "available for reservations booked on or after October 1, 2025")
- **Major Disruptive Events Policy** https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1320 – no date (replaced the Extenuating Circumstances Policy)
- Rebooking and refund policy https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2868 – "Effective Date: February 6, 2025"

### Host cancellation fees (990, quoted)
"we will impose fees subject to a minimum cancellation fee of $50 USD":
- "If the reservation is canceled 48 hours or less before check-in, or after check-in, the fee is 50% of the reservation amount for the nights not stayed"
- "If the reservation is canceled anytime between 48 hours and 30 days before check-in, the fee is 25% of the reservation amount"
- "If the reservation is canceled more than 30 days before check-in, the fee is 10% of the reservation amount"
- 28+ night stays: fee is a percentage "of the non-refundable portion of the reservation ... up to the 30-day period following the cancellation date".
- "the reservation amount includes the base rate, cleaning fee, and any pet fees, but excludes taxes and guest fees. If the calculated cancellation fee is less than $50 USD, it will be adjusted up to $50 USD."
- Fees "withheld from the next payout(s)"; plus "hosts who cancel ... will not receive a payout for the canceled reservation".

**Other consequences:** "blocking the Listing's calendar" for the dates; "hosts may have their Listing or account suspended or removed, and may lose their Superhost status."

**Host "found responsible" even if the guest cancels:** "when it occurs because of conditions in the Listing that are grossly and materially different from how the Listing was described at the time of booking ... Examples may include: double-booking a Listing, substituting another property for the Listing booked by the guest, or gross Listing inaccuracies that materially disrupt a guest stay".

**Fee waivers:** "if the host cancels because of a Major Disruptive Event or certain valid reasons beyond the host's control. Hosts who believe one of these situations applies will be required to provide documentation or other support." 4117 lists valid reasons: "emergency repairs to your home, a serious illness that prevents hosting, evidence that a guest intends to break a house rule". "In the event a fee is waived, other consequences may still apply, like blocking a Listing's calendar." "Regardless of whether we waive any fees ... the host will not receive a payout". 4117: "you can contact us before you cancel."

**Procedure (166):** "cancel it promptly from your Today tab—then send your guest a courtesy message." "Don't ask or encourage your guest to cancel on your behalf." "Canceling a reservation within 24 hours of check-in, or after a trip has started, is a special situation—you'll need to contact us for help with that." Guest gets "a full refund" and rebooking help (2868: travel credit or refund).

**Guest-side cancellation policies the host chooses (475, short stays ≤27 nights):**
- All include a 24-hour free cancellation "as long as the reservation was confirmed at least 7 days before check-in".
- Flexible: "Guests can cancel until 24 hours before check-in for a full refund including taxes, and you won't be paid. If they cancel after that ... you'll be paid for each night they stay, plus one additional night".
- Moderate: full refund "until 5 days before check-in"; after that "you'll be paid for one additional night, plus 50% for all unspent nights".
- Limited (bookings from Oct 1, 2025): full refund until 14 days before; 50% between 7 and 14 days; 100% to host under 7 days.
- Firm: full refund until 30 days before; 50% between 7 and 30 days; 100% to host under 7 days.
- Strict / Super strict: "only available to certain hosts by invitation from Airbnb".
- Non-refundable discount option available; "still subject to the 24-hour cancellation period".
- Long-term (28+ nights): Firm or Strict variants (30 nights' payout protection).
- Override: "Your cancellation policy may be overridden ... Major Disruptive Event ... Refund and Rebooking Policy".

**Major Disruptive Events Policy (1320, quoted):** applies "In the rare circumstances that large-scale events prevent or legally prohibit completion of a reservation". Covered Events (must "occur after the time of booking"): "Declared public health emergencies and epidemics" (not COVID-19, not endemic diseases); "Government travel restrictions ... such as an evacuation order or shelter-in-place order" (not advisories); "**Military actions and other hostilities. Acts of war, hostilities, invasions, civil war, terrorism, explosions, bombings, rebellions, riots, and insurrection.**"; "Large-scale outages of essential utilities"; "Unforeseeable natural disasters and severe weather events".
- "we activate the Policy for the impacted area and timeframe ... Reservations outside of the defined area and timeframe may not be eligible, though hosts may still be able to cancel without adverse consequences if they are unable to host."
- Not covered: "Unexpected injury or illness", "Transportation disruptions such as airline insolvency, flight cancellations", "Non-binding travel advisories", events affecting the guest but "not the reservation location".
- Host effect: "hosts can cancel without fees or other adverse consequences ... their listing's calendar will be blocked for the dates ... the host does not receive a payout".
- "Hosts are obligated to cancel a reservation if their listing is uninhabitable or is inconsistent with what the guest booked. Failure to do so may result in listing suspension".
- "any refunds outside of the reservation's cancellation policy are at the host's discretion. Airbnb does not take part in or guarantee such refunds."

### What this means for this host
- A host cancellation costs at least $50 even on a cheap one-night booking (10%/25%/50% of base+cleaning, min $50), plus the lost payout, a blocked calendar and Superhost risk. Avoid it; keep the calendar synced (iCal) so double-bookings never happen – a double-booking is treated as the host's fault even if the guest ends up cancelling.
- If the host genuinely cannot host (illness, repair, guest signals a rule breach), contact Airbnb *before* cancelling with evidence to get the fee waived; the payout is still lost and the dates stay blocked.
- Never ask the guest to cancel instead.
- If missiles/war/evacuation orders affect Tel Aviv, check whether Airbnb has activated the Major Disruptive Events Policy for the area; if yes, both sides can cancel penalty-free (host still unpaid). Rocket sirens without an activation do not automatically qualify; airline cancellations never qualify. Guest illness never qualifies – that falls under the listing's chosen cancellation policy.
- Choose Flexible or Moderate for a 1-guest room unless there is a reason for Firm/Limited; the 24-hour free-cancellation window applies regardless. Any goodwill refund beyond the policy is the host's choice and is done via Airbnb (Resolution Center / contact support), not cash.
- Within 24 hours of check-in or after arrival, cancellations go through Airbnb support, not the app button.

## 1. Messaging rules (links, phone numbers, email; scheduled messages / quick replies)

### 1a. Off-Platform and Fee Transparency Policy
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799
- Last updated: not shown.
- This is the controlling policy. Key verbatim rules:
  - Prohibited: "Including links that take people off of the Airbnb platform in listings or messages" (listed under "moving bookings off of Airbnb" – the context is links used to move bookings/payments off Airbnb).
  - Prohibited: "Asking or encouraging users to move current, future, or repeat bookings... off of Airbnb"; offering discounts for off-platform bookings; cancelling to rebook elsewhere.
  - Prohibited: "Asking guests for contact information prior to booking; all guest communications prior to booking must be on Airbnb".
  - Prohibited: "Soliciting guests for their email, mailing address, or other communications channels using the Airbnb messaging system or email alias after a booking".
  - Prohibited: "Asking guests for contact information after a booking in order to run credit checks or background checks".
  - Prohibited: "Asking guests to send photos of their government ID prior to arrival except where required for legal or compliance reasons".
  - Prohibited: "Selling, sharing, or using guest contact information for marketing communications or signing guests up for contact list".
  - Prohibited: "Asking guests to create a separate account or register on another website besides Airbnb.com for purposes of gaining entry to a listing".
  - Prohibited: "Asking guests to install a third-party app to access a listing; all listings on Airbnb must be accessible to a guest without requiring they have another app or account". Exception: "Keyless entry apps and apps that facilitate a guest's experience during the stay (ex: Sonos, Nest, concierge apps) as long as they are optional".
  - Prohibited: "You may not ask guests to review an Airbnb stay on a non-Airbnb website or fill out a survey regarding an Airbnb stay on a non-Airbnb website" (hotel partners excepted).
  - Allowed: "After accepting a booking, you may ask a guest to confirm that the contact information provided by Airbnb is an appropriate way to get in touch during their trip". Guest-requested alternative communication channels are also listed as permitted.
  - Prohibited: "Requesting, sending, or receiving payments outside of Airbnb is prohibited"; "Failing to use Airbnb's 'Change reservation' tool to collect payment for modifications".
  - Enforcement: "In the event of repeated or severe violations, we may suspend or permanently deactivate a user's listings or account."
- What this means for this host:
  - The welcome message (sent after booking) may contain the stayflorentin.com link and the host's WhatsApp number – post-booking communication and contact info are allowed, and the link is a guest guide, not a tool to move bookings off Airbnb. Keep the site free of any "book direct / cheaper outside Airbnb" wording, otherwise the link itself becomes a violation.
  - Never send the link, phone number or email before the booking is confirmed (inquiry/request stage) – the filter blocks it and the policy forbids it.
  - Phrase the WhatsApp line as an optional extra channel ("you can also reach me on WhatsApp"), not as a requirement, and do not ask the guest for their email/phone/address. Asking for arrival time is fine.
  - Do not require the guest to install any app or create any account to get in (door code must work without it).
  - Do not ask guests for passport/ID photos before arrival (see section 6).
  - Do not ask for reviews on Google/other sites about the Airbnb stay.

### 1b. Why we review messages on Airbnb
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1121
- Last updated: not shown.
- Verbatim: Airbnb "may review or analyze your messages on Airbnb for safety, support, and to provide and improve our services", including "Enforcing our Terms of Service and community policies". The system "may block messages from being sent" or require text removal when it detects "Contact information, references to other sites, external links", content violating the non-discrimination policy, or the "off-platform and fee transparency policy". Reminders appear "when the person you're messaging sends or attempts to send content that may not follow Airbnb's policies—such as asking for your contact information before a booking is confirmed."
- What this means: expect automatic blocking/warnings on links, phone numbers and "references to other sites" mainly pre-booking; after booking these pass (confirmed by community reports, 1e). Message text is machine-reviewed, so wording in quick replies must be clean.

### 1c. Paying and communicating through Airbnb (guest-facing)
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/209
- Last updated: not shown.
- Verbatim: "It's against Airbnb policy for hosts to ask you to take your booking off of Airbnb" or to communicate via WhatsApp, Facebook, text or other platforms "prior to booking". Optional amenity payments go through the Resolution Center. "If you choose to pay for your booking outside of Airbnb, you will lose the AirCover protection."
- What this means: WhatsApp is explicitly named as a pre-booking no-go; after booking it is not prohibited.

### 1d. Scheduled quick replies / quick replies
- "Create scheduled quick replies and send them to guests automatically": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2897 (no date)
  - Triggers: "a new reservation, check-in, or checkout". "When you create and schedule a quick reply for a listing, it will be applied to current and upcoming reservations"; "sent in the listing's time zone"; "Quick replies aren't scheduled to be sent by default"; hosts "can always choose to skip, customize, or send messages early". Toggle "Send for last minute bookings and short stays": messages scheduled before check-in "will never be skipped–we'll send them at the time of booking if the normal trigger has already passed". With team hosting "all messages will be sent from the team owner"; co-hosts "will be able to skip and edit upcoming scheduled quick replies" but cannot "view or edit your templates".
- "Create and send quick replies to guests": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2898 (no date)
  - "Airbnb messages, including quick replies, have a limit of 4,000 characters." Shortcodes pull guest name, check-in date, house rules etc.; if listing info is missing "the detail will display as unavailable in the message". "Both you and your co-hosts can set up your own quick replies."
- "How temporary phone numbers work": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3764 (no date)
  - Masked numbers are "Currently only available to a limited number of users in the United States and Canada". Where active: "Any exchange of real phone numbers through the Airbnb platform...will be blocked or replaced with the temporary number, when appropriate"; "WhatsApp is not supported with these temporary phone numbers"; guest phone-number shortcuts in quick replies were removed for privacy.
- "Send documents in a message": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3760 (no date)
  - "Attaching and sending documents, like a PDF, directly in messages isn't possible—however, you can share a link to your document if it's hosted on a file-sharing platform such as Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, or WeTransfer." Also: "You shouldn't use email or messaging to ask for guests' contact information before they book a stay, or misuse their contact information."
- "Send photos and videos in a message": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3759 (no date)
  - "Photos and videos can only be sent after a reservation is confirmed." Must "comply with our Content Policy and our other Community Policies."
- "Share recommendations with your guests in messages": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/4101 (no date)
  - Lets hosts attach Airbnb Experiences/Services to messages or scheduled quick replies "at the time of booking or a few days before check-in". Only Airbnb-hosted experiences/services; no mention of third-party links.
- What this means: scheduled messages are a supported feature; the only hard limits are 4,000 characters and that anything scheduled "on booking" is by definition post-confirmation, so links/phone numbers in it are allowed. Airbnb explicitly points to Google Drive/Dropbox links for documents, which confirms external links to a guest document are an accepted practice after booking. Set "Send for last minute bookings" on so the door-code message is never skipped. Israel is not in the masked-phone-number programme, so the host's real number goes through.

### 1e. Content Policy
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/546 (no date)
- Applies to "messages to Airbnb, hosts, or guests" as well as listings, profiles, reviews. Prohibited: "Content, including superimposed company logos, links, or company names, created for the sole purpose of advertising a separate business or other commercial entity unrelated to the listing"; "Spam, unwanted contact, or content that is shared repeatedly in a disruptive manner". Enforcement: Airbnb may "restrict, suspend, or remove the associated Airbnb listing or account".
- What this means: a guest-guide link related to the stay is fine; an Airbnb message whose purpose is to advertise GetYourGuide/Airalo/the spa would not be. Keep affiliate content off Airbnb messages (as is currently the case) and keep the welcome message focused on the stay.

## 2. Host Affiliate Pilot Program and recommending/earning from third-party services

### 2a. Host Affiliate Pilot Program Terms
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3873
- Last updated: March 18, 2025.
- "Select, invited eligible Airbnb Homes hosts" ("Recommending Hosts") earn "$10 USD (or local equivalent) for each Experience that is booked and taken" by their guests during the stay, "up to a maximum of 3 Experiences per each guest's reservation for $30 USD". Links are sent "via in-app messaging". Pilot deadlines: links shared by April 30, 2025; Experiences booked and completed by May 7, 2025. "The guest's reservation with the Recommending Host must not be cancelled". Airbnb "reserves the right to withhold payment if it believes, in its sole discretion, that the Recommending Host or their guest has violated" the Terms; "Program is subject to change or withdrawal at any time and without notice." Participants are responsible for taxes.
- What this means: this was an invite-only pilot that expired in May 2025 and only concerns Airbnb Experiences. It does not cover GetYourGuide or any outside affiliate. Its successor is the "Share recommendations" feature (article 4101), which pays nothing but is the sanctioned way to recommend tours inside Airbnb messages.

### 2b. Is recommending third-party tours allowed? Is earning a commission allowed?
- Terms of Service: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2908 – last updated February 5, 2026.
  - "The Airbnb Platform may contain links to third-party websites, applications, services or resources... Airbnb is not responsible or liable for any aspect of such Third-Party Services". Members must "Do not request, make, or accept a booking or any payment outside of the Airbnb Platform to avoid paying fees, taxes or for any other reason" (Offline Fee Policy excepted). Hosts "are responsible and liable...for the acts and omissions of anyone you allow to participate in providing your Host Services." Hosts must comply with local laws.
- Host Privacy Standards: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2862 (no date) – "You may not encourage or require Guests to: open an account, leave a review, or otherwise interact with a third-party website, application or service before, during or after a reservation, unless such third party is approved by Airbnb or reasonably necessary for the Host to provide the requested service."
- Content Policy (546): no advertising of "a separate business or other commercial entity unrelated to the listing" in listings/messages.
- Guidebooks: Airbnb's own Guidebook feature (article 252 and the hosting resource "Recommend local activities with this simple tool") invites hosts to recommend restaurants, shops and activities, i.e. recommending local third-party businesses is encouraged.
- No Help Center article prohibits a host from earning affiliate commissions from third parties, and none permits it explicitly. The constraints that do exist: (1) nothing in Airbnb messages/listing may be advertising unrelated to the listing (546); (2) no encouraging guests to "interact with a third-party website, application or service" beyond what is approved or necessary (2862 – the strict reading covers any link to GetYourGuide/Airalo/spa sent through Airbnb); (3) no payments for the Airbnb reservation itself outside Airbnb (2908/2799) – affiliate payments from GetYourGuide are not reservation payments, so 2799 does not apply to them.
- What this means for this host:
  - Recommending tours: allowed. Safest channel is Airbnb's own tools (Guidebook, "Share recommendations" with Airbnb Experiences).
  - Earning commissions: not prohibited by any Airbnb rule found, as long as the affiliate links stay on stayflorentin.com and are not pushed through Airbnb messages or the listing (current setup). The Host Privacy Standards sentence is the one real exposure: do not word the welcome message as "sign up at X" / "download Airalo"; the site is an optional guide, and the guest chooses to click.
  - Keep the affiliate disclosure on the site (already present for Airalo) – Airbnb does not require it, but consumer law and GetYourGuide/Airalo terms do.
  - Do not let any affiliate flow collect guest personal data via Airbnb (no forms asking for name/email on the guide site tied to Airbnb reservations – see section 6).

## 4. Fees and extra charges, price transparency, taxes

### 4a. Collecting fees outside Airbnb (Offline Fee Policy summary page)
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2827 (no date). Also referenced in ToS 2908 as the "Offline Fee Policy".
- Verbatim: "Hosts aren't permitted to collect any fees related to Airbnb reservations outside of our platform, unless expressly authorized by us." Authorized only for select software-connected hosts: "resort fees (including the cost of amenities like pool, gym, or wifi), utility fees, and HOA fees", if in the price breakdown. "In locations where Airbnb doesn't collect taxes or where hosts are legally required to collect them directly from guests, hosts may collect disclosed taxes off of Airbnb." "Most hosts aren't allowed to charge security deposits." "Hosts must collect payment for optional fees using the Resolution Center, though hotels may collect fees directly."
- What this means: no cash at the door for anything (extra night, late checkout, towels, cleaning). The only cash-type exception possibly relevant in Israel is a legally required tax collected directly and disclosed in the listing – only if the host actually has such an obligation.

### 4b. Off-Platform and Fee Transparency Policy – fee rules
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2799 (no date)
- Verbatim: mandatory fees are "Costs that guests must pay given the number of nights booked, guest count, and pet count. Examples include utility fees, extra guest fees, pet fees, resort fees, management fees, destination fees, homeowners association (HOA) fees, and taxes." "All mandatory fees must be disclosed in the appropriate fee field or the nightly price if there is no applicable fee field." Prohibited: "Failing to use Airbnb's 'Change reservation' tool to collect payment for modifications"; not disclosing security deposits where permitted.
- What this means: extra guest fee must be set in the listing's "extra guest fee" field, cleaning fee in the cleaning-fee field; an extra night or a change in guest count goes through "Change reservation", not the Resolution Center or cash.

### 4c. How the Resolution Center helps you
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/767 (no date)
- Hosts may request money for: agreed refunds; optional services (e.g. "paid transportation service"); disclosed mandatory fees in listed categories (utilities, resort, linen, management, HOA, cleaning); damage. Hosts may NOT use it "for additional costs or provide refunds for modifications to nights, guest count, or pet count" or for undisclosed mandatory fees ("appropriate fee field"). Deadlines: "up to 60 days after your reservation's checkout date" to submit; for Airbnb mediation, issue reported within "72 hours of discovery"; the other party has "1 hour to respond" once mediation is requested (per page as fetched); if no agreement "a dedicated team member will review the info provided by everyone and ask questions (if necessary) before making a final decision."
- Damage: Host damage protection (https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/279) – file a Resolution Center reimbursement request within 14 days of checkout; guest has 24 hours to respond; then escalate to Host Damage Protection. Host Damage Protection Terms (https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2869, last updated August 1, 2026): request form "Within thirty (30) days of the Responsible Guest's checkout date"; exclusions include wear and tear, mold, "losses from war/terrorism", linen stains (limited exceptions), and weapons unless "stored, secured and disclosed in accordance with Airbnb's Community Standards".
- What this means: late checkout fee or any optional extra = Resolution Center request (only if the guest agrees or Airbnb mediates; best to state the late-checkout fee in house rules). Extra guest = Change reservation. Damage = Resolution Center within 14 days, then AirCover within 30 days; take photos within 72 hours. "Losses from war/terrorism" are excluded – relevant in Israel.

### 4d. Add additional fees to home listings
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2385 (no date)
- Standard hosts get three fee fields: cleaning ("A flat fee, fixed per booking"), pet, extra guest ("A flat fee for each additional guest beyond a default number"). "The cleaning fee is shown separately to guests, unless the feature to have your total price is turned on." "Their total price includes your nightly price, any additional fees you set (for cleaning, extra guests, or pets), Airbnb's service fees, and taxes."
- Price display: Airbnb shows guests a total price including fees before taxes (total-price toggle; mandatory in the US). Preview with "Preview your price breakdown" https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3638.
- What this means: whatever the host wants to charge must live in those fields; nothing may be "announced" in the welcome message as an extra charge.

### 4e. Taxes (Israel)
- "Taxes for hosts": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/481 (no date) – "As a host, depending on your location, you may be required to collect local taxes"; "Depending on your country of residence, you may need to account for VAT/GST on the offering you provide"; "You are responsible for submitting, paying, and reporting all custom taxes"; Airbnb collects VAT/GST on its service fees "in countries that tax electronically supplied services". No Israel-specific rule.
- "Areas where tax collection and remittance by Airbnb is available": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2509 – Israel is NOT listed. "Hosts located in these areas are responsible for assessing all other tax obligations"; unlisted-area hosts can add taxes manually.
- "Value Added Tax (VAT) and how it applies to you": https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/436 – Israel not in the country list; hosts "may need to assess VAT...on the accommodation and/or experience services you provide to guests".
- Terms of Service 2908: "As a Host, you are responsible for determining and fulfilling your obligations under applicable laws to report, collect, remit, or include in your price any applicable VAT or other indirect taxes, occupancy taxes, tourist, income, or other taxes".
- Privacy Policy 3175 (last updated February 5, 2026): for accommodation "located in Israel or the Palestinian Territories, on or after April 20, 2026, your controller is Airbnb Limited LLC located at 888 Brannan Street, San Francisco". (Data-controller change, not a tax rule.)
- No "Responsible hosting in Israel" article exists in the Help Center (searched; only other countries have one).
- What this means: Airbnb collects and remits nothing for Israel. Israeli income tax/VAT on the rental income is entirely the host's responsibility (Israeli rules, outside Airbnb's docs). If any tax must be charged to guests, it must be shown in the listing (custom tax / nightly price), not collected in cash unannounced.

## 5. Guest safety and emergency requirements

### 5a. Safety requirements for homes
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2904 (no date)
- Verbatim: "Maintain smoke and carbon monoxide (CO) detectors. Hosts should clearly state whether these devices are present in a listing." "Hosts must disclose any safety hazards inherent to the listing by including them in the listing description." "Keys or access codes should be provided for the main point of entry. Lockboxes and entry codes must remain secure, and hosts should change codes between each reservation." Hosts must "disclose and take safety measures to secure weapons" present at listings; "Hosts must follow relevant laws and safety regulations, such as applicable fire code requirements." Not mentioned as requirements: fire extinguisher, first-aid kit, emergency contacts, safety card.
- What this means: (1) smoke detector should exist and the listing's safety section must say truthfully whether smoke/CO alarms exist; (2) any hazard (steep stairs, balcony, etc.) goes in the listing description; (3) the door code should be changed between guests – sending a fixed code in every scheduled message is below Airbnb's stated standard; ideally rotate it and update the quick reply, or use a per-guest code.

### 5b. Promoting the safety of our community (Community Policy)
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3063 (no date)
- Prohibited: "Physical violence, sexual assault, or other sexual misconduct", "Other forms of violence, harassment, or threats", drugs, "Disruptive or open-invite gatherings", "Listings with safety hazards (like high fall hazards or exposed wiring)", "Unsecured or undisclosed dangerous animals", "Unsafe or illegal handling of weapons". Emergency: contact local emergency services immediately; Airbnb has a 24-hour safety line for non-emergencies. Airbnb provides "safety guidelines, checklists, emergency preparedness training, and first aid resources" to hosts.
- Safety tips for hosts of places to stay: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/231 – "In an emergency situation, or if your personal safety is threatened, contact local police or emergency services immediately"; "always pay and communicate on Airbnb"; complete house rules and guest manuals.
- Safety card / first aid (written for Experience hosts but the resources are generic): https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2622 and https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2604 – Red Cross/IFRC "safety cards that Hosts can provide to guests, and potentially even fill out on guests' behalf, so that everyone has quick access to local emergency information" (Personal, Indoor, Outdoor Safety Cards); hosts are "recommended to prepare an emergency plan".
- Weapons: Host Damage Protection Terms 2869 – weapons "including but not limited to standard firearms, air guns, self-defense or deterrent devices such as tasers or pepper spray, ammunition of any kind and imitation firearms" are not covered unless "stored, secured and disclosed in accordance with Airbnb's Community Standards". Safety requirements 2904: weapons must be disclosed and secured.
- Israel-specific: no Help Center article on Israel sirens/shelters (searched "Israel emergency safety sirens shelter"; nothing). Only general guidance: emergency plan, local emergency numbers, safety card.
- What this means: Airbnb does not mandate a first-aid kit, extinguisher or safety card for homes, but recommends an emergency plan and safety card. For Tel Aviv the sensible implementation is a page on stayflorentin.com / printed card with: emergency numbers (100/101/102), nearest shelter or protected space and what to do on a siren, host's phone, building address for ambulance, location of fire extinguisher. If the host keeps any weapon or pepper spray in the apartment it must be locked away and disclosed in the listing.

## 6. Privacy – guest personal data

### 6a. Host Privacy Standards
- URL: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2862 (no date shown)
- Verbatim: "As a Host you will receive and use Guests' personal information to manage your reservations and deliver your Host Service." "You are responsible for complying with applicable privacy laws when you handle and process personal information." "You should only use personal information you receive through the Airbnb Platform as necessary to manage your reservations, comply with applicable laws, and deliver your Host Service." "You may not encourage or require Guests to: open an account, leave a review, or otherwise interact with a third-party website, application or service before, during or after a reservation, unless such third party is approved by Airbnb or reasonably necessary for the Host to provide the requested service." Cross-border: data from EEA/Switzerland/UK is covered by GDPR Standard Contractual Clauses (host as importer); data categories include "the Guest's profile and full name, the full name of any additional Guests (if entered), the Guest's cancellation history, Guest's phone number, any other information the Guest chooses to share... messages exchanged with the Guest"; Annex II requires "appropriate technical and organisational measures" (encryption, resilience, incident recovery, regular assessment).
- What this means: guest names/phones may be used only to run the stay. Do not pass guest names to the spa, GetYourGuide, a cleaner's WhatsApp group, or any tool beyond what is needed; do not add guests to mailing lists; delete WhatsApp chats/photos of guests when no longer needed. European guests' data (most of this host's guests) is formally under GDPR clauses – keep it minimal and secure.

### 6b. ID / passport copies
- Off-Platform policy 2799: "Asking guests to send photos of their government ID prior to arrival except where required for legal or compliance reasons" is prohibited; "Selling, sharing, or using guest contact information for marketing communications" is prohibited.
- Booking requirements for reservations: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1170 (no date) – hosts may request ID "after booking if their listing mentions it is required for legal or compliance reasons"; "Airbnb doesn't share your government ID with hosts when you book"; some regions require guest registration with local authorities, done "directly with the local authorities or at the accommodation by the host". Verifying identity (https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1237): a host "may ask for ID upon check-in, if done in person and if the requirement is in the Host's house rules at the time of booking or is required under applicable laws"; hosts "cannot ask a booking guest to submit their ID through the messaging feature unless they have legal or compliance reasons".
- Why some guest profiles show limited information: https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3894 – Airbnb itself limits what hosts see.
- What this means: do not ask for passport photos in the welcome message or on WhatsApp. If Israeli law requires a guest register (check locally), state it in house rules and look at the ID in person at check-in rather than keeping copies. Never photograph/keep IDs unless legally required, and then store securely and delete when the obligation ends.

### 6c. Terms of Service – content and messaging
- ToS 2908: Members must "Do not use bots, crawlers, scrapers, or other automated means to access or collect data"; Airbnb may review messages (1121) and "never review, scan, or analyze your messaging communications to send you third-party marketing messages".
